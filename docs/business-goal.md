# Cognitive Development Business — Mission Reference

This is a **reference skill, not a task skill**. Its job is to give a shared
understanding of *why* the business exists, *who* it's for, and *what makes a
product on-mission* — so that product scoring, trend forecasting, and competitor
research all pull in the same direction instead of drifting toward "whatever is
trending" or "whatever is cheap to import."

Read this skill fully before running any product-finding, product-scoring,
trend-forecasting, or competitor-research work for this business. When other
scoring approaches conflict with the philosophy here, treat this skill as the
tiebreaker.

## The problem this business exists to solve

Children in Bangladesh are spending hours a day on mobile screens (short-form
video, mobile games). This is shrinking their attention span and, over time,
their patience and tolerance for effortful, non-instant-gratification tasks.
The business's founding bet: this is a real and worsening problem for parents,
and a market opportunity for products that visibly counteract it.

## The thesis

Hands-on, physical play — starting with **wooden toys** — builds patience and
logical/critical thinking in a way screens structurally cannot, because:

- Physical objects don't reward the passive, infinite-scroll attention pattern
  that screens do. A puzzle piece doesn't auto-advance.
- Manipulating something with your hands (fitting, stacking, sorting, building)
  forces sustained, sequential attention — which *is* patience, practiced.
- Logical/critical thinking activities have a natural difficulty curve that
  screens rarely enforce; a wooden puzzle can't be "beaten" by mashing a button.

**Wooden toys are the entry point, not the whole business.** The expansion path:
wooden toys that build logical/critical thinking → broader catalog of products
that build **problem-solving skills** (STEM/logic/math and similar). Every new
category should trace back to the "builds patience through effortful, hands-on
cognition" thesis — not drift toward generic educational products or trending
toys just because they sell.

## Target audience

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| End user | Children under 18, Bangladesh. Needs a plausible specific age fit, not "ages 3–15." |
| Buyer | Parents — actual purchaser and primary marketing audience. Motivated by concern about screen time and attention span, not novelty. |
| Channels | Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, personal website (F-commerce). |

## The mission lens — how to evaluate any product

Ask these questions in order when scoring or shortlisting:

1. **Does this fight passive screen attention, specifically?** Low-effort/low-attention "educational" products (passive flashcards) are weaker than those demanding sustained hands-on effort (multi-step build, puzzle).
2. **Does it have a genuine difficulty curve?** On-mission products get harder to master, not "solved once and shelved." This is what builds patience over repeated play.
3. **Is it physical and hands-on?** Digital/app-based "educational" products are off-mission by default — the whole point is to be the antidote to screens.
4. **Does it fit the expansion logic?** A product should be explainable in one sentence as "this builds [specific skill] the same way our wooden toys build [specific skill]." If that sentence is a stretch, flag it as speculative.

This lens is a **qualifier layered on top of** commercial/trend/margin/shipping
scoring — it doesn't replace that scoring, but a product that scores well
commercially and poorly on this lens should be explicitly flagged as
"off-mission," not silently ranked high.

## Product roadmap

| Phase | Scope |
|---|---|
| **Phase 1 (current)** | Wooden toys building logical/critical thinking — puzzles, building sets, sorting/matching, construction toys. |
| **Phase 2 (stated expansion)** | Broader problem-solving products — STEM kits, logic games, spatial-reasoning toys. Not limited to wood; limited to "effortful hands-on cognition." |
| **Future phases** | Sanity-check any new category against the mission lens before treating it as in-scope. Confirm with user; don't quietly expand the roadmap. |

## Brand voice

- Lead with the parent's underlying worry (attention span, screen addiction),
  not just product features. "Give them something better to do with their hands"
  beats "fun new toy."
- Favor visible, demonstrable effort and payoff — a child working through and
  completing something. "Watch my kid solve this" and before/after angles.
- Avoid shaming parents or children about screen use; frame it as building a
  positive skill, not fixing a failure.

## Notes for the researcher

- Silently apply the mission lens as an extra filter on top of any task's own
  output — don't just append this skill's content verbatim.
- If a product scores well commercially but is clearly off-mission (purely
  passive, luck-based), say so explicitly rather than omitting the concern.
- The mission is about *effortful, hands-on cognition*, not "education" as a
  marketing label — keep pushing back on products that only *claim* cognitive
  benefit without a plausible mechanism.
- If the user proposes a category that's a stretch (digital learning apps,
  passive collectibles), flag the mismatch and ask whether they want to formally
  expand the roadmap before treating it as in-scope.
